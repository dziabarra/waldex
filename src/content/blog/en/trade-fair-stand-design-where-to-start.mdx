Fair confirmed, m² booked. Now what? Here's the sequence of steps that **actually works** so you don't end up building your stand 3 days before opening.

## Week 0 — area and budget decision

You usually do this **before** booking with the organizer. But if you already booked without thinking it through, come back to these numbers now:

- **Goal of participation** — B2B lead generation, retail sales, brand awareness, product demonstration? Each goal dictates a different layout.
- **Stand crew size** — typically 1 person per 6-8 m². A 30 m² stand = 4-5 crew (but only 2-3 actively engaged at any time).
- **What you're showing** — physical product? multimedia presentation? live workshop? A demo zone needs 8-15 m² minimum.
- **Budget** — range, total (stand + transport + installation). Without it, no contractor can quote you reliably.

## Week 1-2 — references and moodboard

Before talking to contractors, **gather visual references**. Literally 5-10 photos of other companies' stands:

- 3-5 from **your fair, previous editions** — which stands stood out in your category
- 2-3 outside your industry whose look you'd love to bring to your own (Apple Store, premium car showroom, art gallery)
- 1-2 competitors' stands — as in "**I don't want mine to look like theirs**"

These are inputs that speed up every conversation with a designer 3×. Without references, the designer guesses, makes 3 visualizations, you don't like them, makes 3 more, still off, and that's a week wasted.

## Week 2-3 — contractor brief

A short document (1-2 A4 pages):

- **Goal of participation** + KPIs (e.g., "200 MQL leads", "5 demos per day")
- **Area, shape, corner/island** — ideally a drawing from the fair organizer
- **Crew size + roles** (sales, demo, reception, stand manager)
- **Zone allocation** — reception, conversation area, demo, storage, espresso machine
- **Brand assets** — logo (SVG/AI), KV if you have one, color palette, typography
- **Visual references** — Pinterest/Behance link or attachment
- **Budget range** + opening date
- **Constraints** — e.g., "no sofa" / "must have full TV wall"

[We have a brief template](/en/blog/brief-dla-wykonawcy-stoiska) if you'd rather not start from a blank page.

## Week 3-4 — picking the contractor

You contact 2-4 contractors. After your brief, you should receive:

- **Preliminary quote** in 1-2 business days (with assumptions)
- **2-3 initial concepts** as 3D visualizations (this stage can be paid for very large stands above 100 m²)
- **Schedule** — when final design, when production sign-off, when on-site installation

**Red flags** at this stage:
- Contractor doesn't ask about **any details from the brief** and quotes off the cuff — it'll be unreliable
- Quote **without breakdown** by line items (design, materials, transport, installation) — you can't verify any of it
- No reference projects in their portfolio — either they have 3 stands total, or they're hiding something

## Week 4-5 — concept and shape approval

The chosen contractor delivers 2-4 3D visualizations. Your job:

- **Shape approval** — proportions, zone layout. This is where you push for changes if something doesn't work.
- **Materials approval** — wood vs laminate, PVC vs Dibond, warm or cold LED. Material samples at the contractor's office are very helpful here.
- **Color and graphics approval** — this is where the marketing team kicks in (if you have a separate one).

Rule: **everything you approve at this stage is locked for production**. Changes later cost money (each change = new visualization + new material cut).

## Week 5-7 — manufacturing

Contractor cuts, prints, lacquers, pre-assembles. Your role:

- Sign off on **final graphics** (print proof before large-format)
- Deliver your products to the stand (if showing physical) — packaging, transport labels
- Names list with hall access (organizers often require a named list)

## Week 7-8 — logistics

This is the **most overlooked phase**. Check:

- **Installation slot** with the organizer — when the contractor's crew can enter the hall
- **Material delivery slot** — when the truck can pull up to the hall
- **Vehicle passes** for the installation crew
- **Catering** for the installation crew (a 4-person crew over 2 days needs to eat somewhere)
- **Hotel** for the crew if the fair is outside the contractor's home city

## Opening day minus 2 days — on-site installation

Contractor has the stand fully tested at their facility, dismantles, transports, installs at the hall. You show up **the day before opening** to:

- Approve the final result
- Make last visual corrections (move plants, lights, monitor)
- Verify AV (all screens working, WiFi/network)
- Deliver final marketing materials (flyers, samples)

## Opening day

You drink coffee, greet the first clients. The stand is up because you started 8 weeks ago, not 3.
