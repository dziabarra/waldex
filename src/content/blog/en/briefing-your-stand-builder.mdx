The brief determines **70% of the offer quality** you'll receive from a contractor. Without a brief, the quote is "off the cuff" — useless for comparison. With a good brief you get solid quotes in 1-2 days from 3-4 contractors and can choose rationally.

Below is the template we share with clients — usually fits on **1-2 A4 pages**.

## Section 1 — fair facts

The quote makes no sense without these:

- **Fair name** (e.g., Plastpol 2026)
- **Location and dates** (e.g., Targi Kielce, 26-29 May 2026)
- **Hall number + stand number** (if you know it yet)
- **Area** (m²) and shape — corner / island / row / depth
- **Installation slot** — when the organizer lets contractors enter the hall (usually 2 days before opening, sometimes 1)
- **Organizer constraints** — max stand height (usually 4-5 m), power, internet

You'll get most of this in an email from the fair organizer after paying for the booking. **Attach that email to the brief** — the contractor needs the hall plan anyway.

## Section 2 — goal and KPIs

Where the contractor learns **what the stand is supposed to do**:

- **Participation goal** — pick the dominant one: B2B lead generation, sales, brand awareness, product demo, recruitment
- **KPIs** — if measurable, e.g.: "200 MQL leads", "5 demos per day", "letters of intent from 3 companies"
- **Funnel role** — is the fair the first touch (top of funnel) or deal closing (bottom)?
- **Target audience** — who walks the floor, who you want to reach (job title, industry, decision power)

This affects **stand layout**. An awareness stand has a big banner and open space. A demo stand has a closed zone with screen and seating. A recruitment stand has coffee and swag full of QR codes.

## Section 3 — crew and zones

The contractor needs to know **how to design the people flow**:

- **Number of people** on the stand simultaneously (max + typical)
- **Roles** — sales, demo, reception, stand manager, VIP guest
- **Zones** — what's needed:
  - reception / first contact
  - 1:1 client conversation area
  - product demo zone
  - storage (flyers, swag, clothes)
  - kitchenette / espresso machine
  - VIP corner — closed zone for serious conversations

## Section 4 — product / what you show

- **Physical product** — dimensions, weight, presentation requirements (display case, podium, lighting)
- **Multimedia** — screen/projection needed? What content? Do you have final video / materials?
- **Live demo** — anything live (workshop, talk, sample run)?
- **Samples** — how many, how big, how packaged

## Section 5 — brand

- **Logo** — preferred format SVG or AI; if only PNG, contractor will recreate but adds €50-115
- **Visual identity** — KV (key visual), Brand Book PDF if you have one, color palette (Pantone + RGB + CMYK)
- **Brand typography** — font names, licenses
- **Tone & feel** — in one sentence: "premium / industrial / friendly / corporate / startup-y"

## Section 6 — visual references

The strongest element of the brief. Attach:

- **3-5 photos of other companies' stands** you like (from Pinterest, Behance, IG, or previous fairs)
- **1-2 photos of competitors' stands** — as in "**I don't want this**" (brutal but fast)
- **2-3 references outside trade fairs** — interior (Apple Store, premium showroom, gallery, restaurant) that captures the feel you want

The contractor **glances at references and already knows** whether we're going "modular white + accent color" or "wood + copper + premium design".

## Section 7 — budget and timeline

- **Net/gross budget** — a range, e.g., €14,000-21,000 net. This is NOT "how much I want to spend" — it's **the frame the proposal should fit**
- **Fair opening date** (specific day)
- **Expected final design date** (when you want approved design)
- **Decision-making** — who approves the design? How many revision rounds?

## Section 8 — constraints / must have / nice to have

- **Must have**: things without which the offer is rejected (e.g., "must have mezzanine", "must fit espresso machine", "must have an 8-seat table")
- **Nice to have**: extras under consideration (e.g., "wall projection would be nice", "mobile-printed counter would be good")
- **Hard constraints**: what you don't want (e.g., "no music", "no swag on the counter")

## What does NOT need to be in the brief

- Specific materials (wood vs aluminum) — leave it to the contractor, they know the trade-offs
- Specific furniture dimensions — "tall counter for 2 people + low sofa for 4 people" is enough
- Graphic details (font weight of a specific text) — that's marketing's job after the shape is approved

```
- Name: [...]
- Date: [...]
- Hall / number: [...]

- Installation slot: [...]

GOAL
- Main goal: [lead gen / sales / awareness / demo / recruitment]
- KPI: [...]
- Target audience: [...]

CREW AND ZONES
- People: [...] (max), [...] (typical)
- Roles: [...]
- Zones needed: [...]

PRODUCT

BRAND

REFERENCES

- Final design by: [...]
- Decided by: [...]

MUST HAVE
- [...]

NICE TO HAVE
- [...]

DON'T WANT
- [...]
```
