"Do trade fairs pay off?" — this question comes up at every marketing budget decision. The answer **without numbers** is a subjective impression. The answer **with numbers** is a small table and a decision in 5 minutes.

In this post we show the trade fair stand ROI model we use with clients when planning subsequent editions.

## What goes into total cost

Sum **everything**, including the costs that are easy to forget:

| Item | Example: 30 m², domestic fair |
|---|---|
| Stand (design + production + installation + dismantle) | €11,500 |
| Exhibition fee (m² + registrations) | €2,800 |
| Material transport + presentation gear | €700 |
| Crew hotel (4 people × 3 days × €80) | €960 |
| Per-diems, local travel, contingency | €460 |
| Sales team time (4 people × 4 days × €350/day as opportunity cost) | €5,600 |
| Marketing materials (flyers, swag, samples) | €1,150 |
| **Total** | **~€23,200** |

Note that **the stand is only ~50%** of total cost. The sales team + fees + logistics together add up to a lot too.

## ROI calculation — lead-based model

The cleanest approach for B2B:

```
ROI = (Won deals from leads × Margin) − Total cost
       ──────────────────────────────────────────
                       Total cost
```

A real example:

- Fair brought **120 MQLs** (Marketing Qualified Leads — left contact details, in target audience)
- MQL → SQL conversion: **35%** = 42 leads
- SQL → customer: **20%** = 8 new customers
- Average first-order value: **€10,500**, 30% margin = **€3,150 profit** per customer
- Total profit: 8 × €3,150 = **€25,200**

ROI = (€25,200 − €23,200) / €23,200 = **+8%**

So in year one you barely cover costs. **This is where real ROI begins** — because:

- A customer acquired at a fair stays on average 3-5 years (LTV)
- Year two: more orders from these 8 customers + their referrals
- Year-two stand cost is 30-40% of year one (modular) → costs drop

After **three years**, ROI on the same customer cohort can reach 200-400%.

## 5 metrics that actually measure effectiveness

Beyond the bottom-line €:

### 1. Cost per lead (CPL)

`Total cost / number of MQLs`. For our example: €23,200 / 120 = **€193/lead**.

A lot? A little? Compare with other channels:
- B2B Google Ads in central Europe: typically €45-180/MQL
- LinkedIn Ads: typically €90-350/MQL
- Cold outbound: €35-140/MQL (but lower quality)

Trade fairs usually fall in the middle but **lead quality is dramatically higher** — these are people who physically showed up, stayed 15 minutes, and left their details.

### 2. Conversion rates (MQL → SQL → customer)

From fairs, typically 25-40% MQL → SQL and 15-25% SQL → customer. Online conversions are usually 2-3× lower.

### 3. 90-days-post-fair pipeline value

Sum of potential deals × probability of close. A number you can show the CFO **3 months after the fair** before all deals close.

### 4. Cost of presence / m² / day

For 30 m² × 4 days × €23,200 cost = **€193/m²/day**. Useful when deciding "20 m² for 4 days vs 30 m² for 3 days" at the same budget.

### 5. Brand metrics (before vs after)

Harder to measure but real:
- Site traffic from organizer geo (pre/during/post-fair)
- Branded search in Google Trends
- Mentions in industry social media
- Direct contacts to corporate email/phone in the 30 days after

## When ROI **doesn't work**

Trade fairs aren't always the right investment. Four situations where it's better to **skip** or scale down:

1. **Your industry has no buyer at fairs** — if the decision is made elsewhere (e.g., public procurement), there's no point walking the floor.
2. **Average deal value is low** (under €1,200) — you'd need an absurd number of leads to recoup costs.
3. **Sales team has no follow-up process** — fair leads that sit in the inbox for 2 weeks die (44% never get a response from the contractor).
4. **The stand has bad layout** — closed, no demo zone, no conversation space. Then people walk past instead of stopping.
